## What you may do

* Read, quote, and link to the material for personal study, teaching, or
  commentary, consistent with fair use and the source's terms.
* Reuse **original code** under the MIT license with attribution.
* Reuse **original writing** with attribution.

## What you may not do

* Redistribute textbooks, case studies, lecture slides, or exam keys as if they
  were yours, or use them commercially.
* Re-upload the archive as a substitute for buying or licensing course
  materials.
* Remove copyright notices or attribution from third-party files.

## Takedown

If you are a rights holder and want a file removed, please contact the
maintainer (see the GitHub profile) or open an issue that identifies the file.
Files will be removed promptly; because they are in git history, a history purge
can be arranged as well.

## A note on AI-generated content

Some notebooks and reports were produced with the help of AI assistants, as
allowed by the relevant course. They are labeled in the original submissions
where required. The MIT license applies to the code as published here; check
your own course's academic-integrity rules before reusing it.
